# LIQWD — Pricing & Packaging Blueprint (agent tier)

**Status: PROPOSED 2026-09-02, for founder review. Nothing here is wired to
Stripe until the founder picks the numbers.** Source: founder direction
(free = LIQWD-branded; paid ≈ $99 = branded website on your own domain plus
cheap-to-us features; premium above it = all the bells and whistles; stack
lead-gen lanes and other features; go after the leaders in the space), plus
a pricing sweep of the leaders done the same day.

All prices below are **monthly** unless stated. Competitor prices are as
published or reported in September 2026 and are mostly **USD**; Canadian
providers are marked. Sources at the end.

sits.

Public estimates run $300–1,500; the quoted numbers are the ones to price against |

Two things fall out of the table:

1. **Under $130 you get a DIY builder. Above $300 you get done-for-you, but
   paid for with humans** — account managers, designers, ad buyers — which is
   why it comes with setup fees, contracts, and "spend $500+ on ads on top".
   Nobody credible sells done-for-you marketing at $99–299 because nobody
   else can make it without people. LIQWD's engine is automation with an
   approval queue. We can deliver the top band's *outputs* at the bottom
   band's *cost structure*. That is the whole play.
2. **Lock-in is the industry's business model.** Setup fees, annual terms,
   the site lives on their platform, the domain is often theirs, the data
   export is painful. The trust posture of the cheap tier (no setup, cancel
   anytime) combined with the outputs of the expensive tier is a pitch none
   of them can match without breaking their own economics.
3. **The category is bought on competitors' brand names.** The founder's own
   search trail (2026-09-02) shows Agent Image and Real Geeks running ads on
   the keyword "luxury presence", and myRealPage on "web 4 realty". Search
   at the top of this market is contested and expensive, and the incumbents'
   own reviews say where it hurts: weak SEO plumbing under pretty sites,
   unclear ROI for solo agents, five-figure first years. Those are the exact
   sentences our comparison page should open with.

Two Canada-specific facts that sharpen it:

- **TRREB IDX feed costs the vendor nothing. VOW is $1,500/yr, billed to the
  brokerage.** So a resale-search website (IDX) is near-zero marginal cost
  for LIQWD once we hold vendor status — competitors charge $25/mo for the
  privilege. VOW is the one that costs real money, and it belongs in an
  add-on that passes the board fee through.
- The Canadian providers are **website builders**, not marketing engines,
  and the US engines ignore **RECO advertising rules and CASL**. A
  compliance-native, done-for-you marketing engine for Canadian agents does
  not exist at any price. Add the new-homes marketplace as a lead *source*
  no competitor has, and the positioning writes itself.

| Homeowner dashboard with automated home-value updates | R5 past-client nurture + R6 monthly market numbers | Partial — and deliberately *not* a home-value estimate (CLAIM-2 not-an-appraisal; AVM liability). A monthly "your neighbourhood" update from sourced datasets instead |

| "I ran a quick digital audit of your presence" (the sales opener) | "See what Google thinks of you" free report | **Make it public** — see Part 6 |

Two conclusions:

1. **Speed-to-lead is the one feature we can't ship without.** Their AI SMS
   nurture is the headline of their top tier; our Home already scores the
   agent on reply time, which is an odd thing to measure without offering
   the fix. Add the card (Part 2, Pro: instant text + email reply with three
   qualifying questions; Premium: the conversation continues until handoff).
   CASL: consent captured at the lead form; unsubscribe honoured at the
   infrastructure layer, same suppression list as everything else. Needs a
   Canadian SMS number and carrier registration.
2. **Bundled ad budget is how they get to $1,700 and how they hide the
   split.** Keep our rule (pass-through + fee), but show a *suggested
   starter budget* on the upgrade page so the comparison reads as one
   number: "Premium + $500/mo ads ≈ $1,075 all-in, every dollar itemized" —
   still well under Leads Pro's $1,700 plus $3,500 setup, and the agent
   knows exactly where each dollar went.

## Part 2 — What we're actually selling at each tier

The ladder, in the founder's words: free is LIQWD-branded distribution;
paid is *your brand*; premium is *everything, done for you*.

**Rules that govern the packaging (binding):**

- **Nothing that costs LIQWD per-unit money is unlimited.** Pages, AI
  generations, email sends, ad management — every one has a cap or a
  pass-through. Client hubs and email forwarding are the exceptions because
  they cost effectively nothing.
- **Ad spend is never bundled.** It is the agent's money, passed through to
  the platform, with a management fee on top. Bundling it destroys margin
  and invites disputes about "where did my $99 go".
- **The domain is included and portable.** We register it, they own it, it
  transfers out if they leave. Costs ~CA$10–15/yr, kills the number-one
  objection to hosted sites.
- **Month-to-month, no setup fee, cancel anytime.** This is a weapon against
  every provider above $150, and it costs us nothing because onboarding is
  automated.
- **Free stays honest.** Leads from a free agent's own pages go to that
  agent (positioning blueprint, Part 1). *This reverses today's `/upgrade`
  page, which gates the public profile and lead routing behind Pro.* That
  page predates the positioning doc; the doc wins.

### Free — "On LIQWD"  ·  $0

The distribution engine. Everything carries LIQWD's look and lives on LIQWD
property.

- Public agent page on `liqwd.ca` in LIQWD's design, verified badge
- Leads from your own pages and links routed to you, forever
- Up to **3** project lead pages · **2** client hubs
- Brand kit (photo, logo, bio, brokerage) — used by everything above it
- Browse projects, broker commissions, submit projects, the marketplace,
  rental referrals, refer & earn
- One free "what Google thinks of you" report (the taste of the paid GBP
  service)

### Pro — "Your brand"  ·  $99 / mo (founder's anchor)

The branded website tier. One visible object worth paying for monthly, plus
features that are cheap for us and feel expensive to them.

- **Your own branded website:** your colours, your design, your domain
  (included, portable), hosted and managed, resale IDX search when vendor
  status lands (near-zero cost to us — see Part 1)
- **Custom email address on your domain** (`you@yourdomain.ca`) — forwarding
  to their existing inbox is effectively free for us; a full mailbox is a
  pass-through add-on
- **10** lead pages · **unlimited** client hubs
- Newsletter to your contacts, capped sends (e.g. 2,500/mo)
- **Market a listing** kit — 4 listings/month
- Monthly market snapshot in your brand
- Buyer wants (today's Pro feature)
- Social calendar, lite — 4 posts/month drafted for approval
- **Your blog, written for you** — 2 posts/month; the agent picks topics
  and voice from a suggested plan, approves each post. Technical SEO
  (schema, sitemaps, indexing, freshness) runs silently on the site — a
  line in the website card's body, never a card of its own
- **Reply to every lead in seconds** — instant text + email in the agent's
  voice, three qualifying questions, a heads-up to the agent. Consent at
  the form, CASL suppression shared

### Premium — "All in, done for you"  ·  $499 / mo (founder: don't underprice; floor $399)

Everything on, the caps opened wide, and the modules that only make sense
when LIQWD is running the marketing for them.

**Why not cheaper (founder, 2026-09-02):** Luxury Presence proves agents
pay US$500/mo plus a $2,500 setup for two blogs, a Google profile and
"lead intelligence". An "all in, done for you" tier at $249 reads as thin
next to that, and price is a quality signal in this category. The margin
also has to fund the parts that cost real money — SMS, the journalist
feed, GBP API volume, ad monitoring. At CA$499 we sit roughly a quarter
under Brand+ before their setup fee, with several times the output, and
the gap to Pro at $99 makes Pro the obvious entry and Premium the serious
tier rather than "Pro plus a bit".

- Everything in Pro
- **25** lead pages (vs 10) — the number that makes the tier feel like a
  different product, at a marginal cost to us of tokens and an approval
- **All lead-gen lanes** (Part 3) with campaigns launched paused for approval
- Google Business Profile **managed weekly** + review replies (the priced
  Local service — never bundled below this tier)
- Listing presentations, full social calendar, monthly "what worked" story
- Developer deals (today's Ultra feature)
- "What does AI say about you" monthly
- Blog weekly + neighbourhood guides (the agent's own topical network on
  their domain)
- **Get quoted in the news** — journalist-request matching with a
  Vault-grounded draft answer the agent sends, plus press-style posts for
  real announcements only (new brokerage, verified award, market report)
- **Lead nurture by text until handoff** — the instant reply keeps the
  conversation going (availability, timeline, budget band) and hands the
  lead to the agent the moment it's warm; every message logged, every
  claim Vault-grounded
- Past-client "your neighbourhood this month" update from sourced
  datasets — never a home-value estimate
- Priority support, first access to new modules

## Part 2b — SEO & PR: cards versus plumbing (founder question, 2026-09-02)

The dissected suites hold a lot of SEO and PR machinery. The rule for
keeping the plan clean: **an agent gets a card only for something they
decide about. Everything else is plumbing under the website.** Nobody buys
"schema retrofit"; everybody buys "your blog, written for you".

| From the dissection | Becomes | Why |
|---|---|---|
| C1 topical network + C2 production line, per agent | **Card: "Your blog, written for you"** (Pro 2/mo, Premium weekly + neighbourhood guides) | The agent curates topics, neighbourhoods and voice from a suggested plan and approves each post. Their own topical network on their own domain is the SEO asset that compounds. |
| P3 press studio + P1 earned editorial, adapted for agents | **Card: "Get quoted in the news"** (Premium) | Two honest mechanics: (1) journalist-request matching — when a reporter needs a real-estate source, draft a Vault-grounded answer the agent sends; (2) press-style posts for *real* announcements only (P3's rule). No wire-service spend by default; distribution = their site, LIQWD's newsroom, GBP post, socials, and a P1-style pitch to local outlets. |
| O1 page optimizer, O2 schema, O3 indexing, C3 freshness/decay, C4 link weaver, V2 llms.txt | **Plumbing** — one line in the website card's body ("search-ready underneath") | Runs automatically on every LIQWD-rendered page. Surfacing it as cards would be the kvCORE failure mode. |
| E1–E5 Explorer (GSC-driven), V3 gap/trophy | **Folded into "Watch it work"** — the monthly story | "What's working on your site, what we changed, what's next" in plain language. The Explorer tools are the engine; the story is the product. |
| P2 recruitment outreach, C5 authority pages, P4/E6 authority plan | **Stay admin-only** | These grow LIQWD itself. Not agent-facing. |
| Deployed link networks, cloud stacks, paid placements, gray-area indexers | **Refused** (README invariant 7) | Not relitigated. |

Net effect on the plan: **two new cards, not eight.** Blog under "Listings &
content", Get quoted under "The edge". Both added to the catalog the same
day.

Compliance notes that ride with them: blog posts inherit CLAIM-1/2 (every
number sourced; not-an-appraisal); quotes and announcements inherit CLAIM-3
(no invented awards or transactions — a "verified award" means one in the
Vault); outreach to journalists follows the Authority blueprint's CASL and
value-first rules; the machine never converses with a journalist on its
own.

## Part 3 — Lead-gen lanes (the founder's "stack all of that together")

A **lane** is one packaged outcome: a landing-page template + an offer + ad
creative + a nurture sequence + a compliance profile. Lanes are the unit
agents understand ("I want seller leads"), and the unit we can meter.

| Lane | Offer on the page | Nurture | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Resale sellers | home-value report, "what's my home worth" | 6-touch CASL-compliant email/SMS | the highest-value lane; CLAIM-2 not-an-appraisal framing enforced |
| Buyers | curated search, first-time-buyer guide, calculator | drip + saved-search alerts (needs IDX/VOW) | pairs with the branded site's search |
| New homes / condos | project pages, VIP access, floor-plan drops | project-update drip | the lane LIQWD already owns; leads also from the marketplace |
| Listing (pre-listing) | "just listed / coming soon" pages per listing | open-house follow-up | ties to Market a listing (R2) |
| Investors / rentals | pre-con investment guide, rental-referral hooks | monthly investor note | feeds Rental referrals |

Packaging: Pro includes **one** lane (their choice); Premium includes all;
Pro can add a lane for $29/mo. Ads on any lane are the managed-spend add-on
with the hard gate (approved landing page + working tracking) from the
positioning blueprint.

**On "jack it up to 20+ pages":** yes, and it's nearly free for us — but sell
it as **distinct pages** (a lane × a neighbourhood × an offer), not copies.
Google punishes near-duplicate pages, and the topical-network gate
(migrations-spec 0003: metadata-before-generation, node budgets) already
enforces distinctness. Quotas are per page; ads are per campaign.

Rough per-agent monthly cost, to keep the packaging honest:

| Cost line | Free | Pro | Premium |
|---|---|---|---|
| Domain (amortized) | — | ~$1 | ~$1 |
| Hosting / edge | ~$0 | ~$0 | ~$0 |
| AI generation (Opus for the website once; Sonnet/Haiku for everything recurring) | <$1 | $3–8 | $10–25 |
| Email sends (capped) | ~$0 | <$1 | $1–3 |
| GBP / review / social APIs | — | — | ~$0 (API) |
| Ad management | — | — | 0 human hours; fee covers API + monitoring |
| **Gross margin** | n/a | **≈90%** | **≈85–90%** |

The margin lives in the automation. The only way to lose it is to promise
something a human has to do — so no "dedicated account manager" on any tier.
Premium's "priority support" is a response-time promise, not a person.

## Part 5 — Repricing path (today → this ladder)

Today: Free / Pro $9.99 / Ultra $19.99 (Stripe), with Pro gating the public
profile, lead routing, 10 landing pages, and Buyer wants; Ultra adding
Developer deals.

**Blast radius, checked 2026-09-02: zero paying subscribers.** The database
holds 17 free realtors, one developer, and one admin flagged `pro` with no
Stripe subscription. Repricing costs nothing today and gets more expensive
with every paid signup — do it before the first paid customer, not after.

1. **Grandfathering policy** (moot today, keep for later): any paid
   subscriber keeps their price for 12 months and receives the *new*
   entitlements of the matching tier (Pro→Pro, Ultra→Premium).
2. **Move public page + lead routing to Free** (positioning doc). Announce
   it as a gift to every agent on the platform — it is the distribution
   engine.
3. **Rename Ultra → Premium** in the UI (`plan = 'ultra'` stays in the
   database; only labels change). Ultra as a word says "more expensive Pro";
   Premium says "the whole thing".
4. **Entitlements become data**, not scattered `isPro()` checks: a
   `plan_entitlements` table (plan, feature, limit) read by one helper, so
   the founder can move a feature between tiers or change a cap without a
   deploy. Spec to follow in migrations-spec 0006 once the tiers are set
   (0005 is the lead-automation spine: the bare-bones CRM wiring and
   follow-up sequences behind "Reply to every lead in seconds").
5. Rewrite `/dashboard/upgrade` against this ladder (three columns, the
   lanes as a picker, add-ons as a row of chips, the domain-portability
   promise in the footer).

## Part 6 — How we go after the leaders

Not by matching them feature-for-feature. By making their economics look
absurd:

- **"$99, no setup fee, cancel anytime, your domain is yours."** Against
  Luxury Presence's $1,500–5,000 setup and annual lock, or AgentFire's
  $800–6,500 setup, this is the whole ad.
- **"Done for you, not do it yourself."** Against Placester / RealtyNinja /
  myRealPage: same price, but the marketing is made for you and waits for
  one tap of approval.
- **"Ads that can't waste your money."** Paused-first launches, hard caps,
  the landing-page gate, a management fee instead of a $500 minimum. Against
  Sierra / Real Geeks / AgentLocator's human-run PPC.
- **"Built for Ontario."** RECO identification on every piece, CASL on every
  send, TRREB data rules on every listing — silent, automatic. No US platform
  can say it; no Canadian builder does it.
- **"Leads you can't get anywhere else."** The new-homes marketplace feeds
  agents leads no competitor has a source for. Named honestly as one lane
  among several (three-sided thesis), but it is the moat.
- **Steal their opener.** Luxury Presence's rep leads with "I ran a quick
  digital audit of your internet presence." That is our free "See what
  Google thinks of you" report — so put it on the public marketing site as
  the agent-acquisition hook: enter your name and brokerage, get the
  report, create a free account to fix it. It feeds P2 recruitment outreach
  with a warm list and costs one Haiku call per run.
- **Conquest the comparison searches** once Smart Ads is live on LIQWD's own
  account: "luxury presence pricing", "real geeks alternative", "agentlocator
  alternative", "web4realty vs". The incumbents already do this to each
  other. Bidding on a competitor's name as a keyword is allowed; using their
  mark in the ad copy is not — that needs a rule in the rulebook before the
  first campaign. The landing page is Part 1's table with a LIQWD column
  added, and the honest line under it: no setup fee, cancel anytime, your
  domain is yours.
